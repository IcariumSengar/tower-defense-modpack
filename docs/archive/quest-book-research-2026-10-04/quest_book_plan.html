What the best books do

We read the real quest files of the 7 packs installed on this PC: DeceasedCraft, Craft to Exile 2, Suspect's Journey, Cuboid Outpost, Ozone Skyblock Reborn 2, Mechanical Mastery+ and Forever Factory. Craft to Exile 2 runs your exact FTB Quests build. We also pulled the committed books of ATM10, the FTB flagships, FTB Academy, Create: Above and Beyond, Enigmatica, GTNH, Divine Journey 2 and Prominence II from GitHub, and looked at how zombie and tower-defense games teach (Plants vs. Zombies, Kingdom Rush, 7 Days to Die, Fallout). The same eight habits came up again and again.

1 · A road you can read at a glance

One spine of bigger milestone nodes running left to right, with side branches hanging off it where they become relevant. Create: Above and Beyond even keeps a separate overview chapter whose milestones tick themselves off.

And what they get wrong

  • Catalogue bloat. About 375 of DeceasedCraft's 801 quests are untitled "get item X" tiles.
  • Rewards that never change. 905 of Ozone's 967 quests pay exactly one chance cube and one coin.
  • Filler XP. OceanBlock 2 has 869 small XP rewards, and players call them noise.
  • Walls of text. One Divine Journey 2 quest runs to 7,647 characters.
  • Multiplayer duplication. On ATM10's GitHub (issue #3293) a 3-player server complains: "Progression is shared and duplicates can be op."
  • Tick-box onboarding. CTE2's 26-page prologue is almost all "Click Once Read".

The plan in five moves

  1. Keep the one campaign map, and split it into four acts that appear as you hold out.The fishbone stays (your 09-03 and 09-09 calls). Each act gets a banner image that draws only once your team reaches it: Arrival, Dig In, Power, Hordes.
  2. Teach by doing: craft it, then see it work.Every distinct item keeps its own quest. About 35 new quests cover the mechanics the book never mentions, and most of them complete from what actually happens in the world: the first airdrop lands, the first stragglers glow, the first pedestal upgrade is bought.
  3. One page layout for every quest.A characterful title, then a plain subtitle (action, cost and who gets the reward). Page 1 says what it does and how to use it, with every number taken from the code. Page 2 has field notes. Pages end with "Unlocks: …".
  4. Rewards that equip you.A quest pays out the thing you just learned, or the next step's ingredient. Bags climb a tier with each act. Act gates offer a choice of kit, and XP is sized to buy pedestal upgrades.
  5. Multiplayer rules written into the book.Every player claims every reward (your call). Every teaching quest makes each player open it to claim, so nobody skips the lesson. Nothing needs two players. Each player leaves their own amulet to explore.

Why XP changes. Each player can claim 185 levels from the book today. Maxing every pedestal upgrade costs 108 levels, and one player has to hold the levels for each tier. With 3 friends claiming separately that's about 555 levels, five times what the pedestal can absorb. The plan cuts the book's XP to about 60 levels per player, in chunks sized to upgrade tiers and labelled as pedestal fund. Kills keep paying the rest.

Multiplayer

These facts come from FTB Quests 2001.4.22's own code. Agents read the jar's bytecode for them, and each was confirmed independently by two or three of them.

  • A party shares one book. Any member doing a task completes it for everyone, and everyone online gets the completion toast.
  • Rewards are claimed per player by default. A reward marked "team" is claimed once for the whole party, by whoever clicks first.
  • Hand-ins take items from whoever clicks. One player's 8 gold completes "Not Just Jewelry" for the party, and then each player claims their own amulet. That's the behaviour you chose on 09-25.
  • "Have the item" tasks check one inventory at a time. They count the best single inventory, not the party's combined total.
  • Repeatables are first come, first served. One claim of Zombie Masher resets it for the whole party, though its text promises everyone a claim.
  • A friend who joins mid-run sees the party's book. They can claim every per-player reward from quests already done, which works as a free catch-up kit.

Rules the plan adds

  1. Every player claims every reward (decided 10-05). Waves don't scale with player count, so the extra loot in a party is accepted.
  2. Every teaching quest stays manual-claim. The "!" alert pulls each player into the page to claim, so a friend who didn't craft the item still reads how it works.
  3. The repeatable bounty becomes fixed rungs, so the first claim no longer locks the others out of that cycle.
  4. Nothing requires two players. Everything works solo, which is a party of one.
  5. A Field Notes page called "Playing Together". It covers joining one party, who owns placed SecurityCraft defences (only the placer can change them), and how claims work.
Found along the way, not a book issue: no FTB Teams party exists in any current save. The dedicated server's party folder is empty, so right now everyone has separate progress. Invites also need chat clicks, which some of your accounts can't use. The plan works either way, but a party gets the most out of it.

Amulets in co-op (decided 10-05, a game rule outside the book): each player has to leave their own amulet to explore. The stand still shows one amulet, the first one left, which opens the border as now. Everyone else leaves theirs through the pedestal screen, which lists whose amulets are in, and Take only returns your own. The existing per-player push-back then checks your own amulet: anyone whose amulet isn't left there, including players who haven't earned one yet, is held at the real border line.

The other chapters

  • Field Notes (renamed from Tips and Tricks). Keep the key tips and fix three texts: there's no furnace by the crafting table, middle-click sort is still unreliable, and hardcore only ends the run once everyone is dead. Add Playing Together, Who Owns the Defences, Your Own Blasts Are Safe, Beams Mean Loot, Waypoints (K) and Tier Tooltips.
  • The Arsenal. Group the guns under their ammo, so each ammo is the parent of the guns it feeds. Give airdrop-only guns their own shape so they don't read as craftable. Each gun quest pays a stack of its ammo. Fix the SMG (32 rounds, not 60) and the Energized Dust text.
  • Bounties. Keep the 25/100/300/750 ladder and the repeatable. Rename the "Reaper" tier, which clashes with the boss. Pay Shrapnel plus escalating bags, and replace the repeatable with fixed rungs every 1500 kills so every player can claim each one.
  • Challenges (optional, your call). About six spoiler-safe mastery goals for veterans replaying the book on a new world: hold a wave with zero pedestal damage, kill a boss before it reaches the pedestal, max every upgrade, reach Horde 20 in hardcore.

Each phase is sized to fit one session, so a usage limit can't strand one half-built.

Phase 1restructure + rewrite
Acts on the spine and a legend quest. One quest per item: split Flux, RS, IE parts and OMT bases. Fix all 18 text mismatches, apply the reward rules and team flags, and add the new Field Notes pages and the regrouped Arsenal. Decided 2026-10-05

  2. XP: pedestal fund, about 60 levels per player in tier-sized chunks.
  3. Hidden discovery quests: yes, a handful.
  4. Optional field tests: yes, never blocking.
  5. Act banners that reveal as you progress, plus diagrams: yes.

  7. Challenges chapter: yes, as Phase 4.
  8. Co-op amulets: each player leaves their own (see Multiplayer). Players with no amulet are blocked at the border.
The 18 places today's text disagrees with the code
  1. Open It: "every bag can carry Shrapnel". Legendary bags carry none.
  2. Past the Line: the structure task never sees the 6 ruins the base build places itself.
  3. Sentry: "never runs out of arrows". It fires 8-damage bullets.
  4. I.M.S. and Sentry: the targeting text predates the pack's hostiles/mobs-only defaults.
  5. Bouncing Betty: "doesn't care who trips it". Players trigger it, but its blast can't hurt them.
  6. Submachine Gun: holds 32 rounds, not 60 (the Tommy Gun holds 60).
  7. Energized Dust: the recipe still shows in JEI. It's only uncraftable because glowstone can't be found.
  8. Reach Further: the base has no furnace.
  9. Auto-Sort: middle-click sorting was reported broken and never re-checked.
  10. Go Hardcore: the run only ends once every online player is dead.
  11. Zombie Masher: in a party only the first claimer gets each cycle.
  12. Shore It Up: levels can't be pooled, and the chat link only goes to players who can afford a tier.
  13. Fragmentation and Fire for Effect: the subtitles list a Turret Base as an ingredient; it's a separate block.
  14. The Reaper: the bosses alternate (Reaper, then Demolisher), and the name clashes with a Bounty tier.
  15. Find the Pedestal: mining the pedestal also ends the run, and nothing protects it from that.
  16. Borrowed Time: the gear isn't actually worn. That's the hint you asked for, so it stays.
  17. First Blood: deaths by drowning, falling and similar causes don't count.
  18. Spoils of War: pays 2 emeralds, which nothing in the pack uses.
